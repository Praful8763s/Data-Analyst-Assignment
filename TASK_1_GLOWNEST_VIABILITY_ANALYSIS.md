# Task 1: Campaign Viability Analysis - GlowNest Premium Face Serum

## Brand Details
| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Product | Premium Face Serum |
| Selling Price (SP) | ₹800 per unit |
| Cost of Goods Sold (COGS) | ₹280 per unit |
| Amazon Referral Fee | 12% of SP |
| Monthly ad spend | ₹3,00,000 |
| Units sold via ads (monthly) | 1,500 |
| Organic units sold (monthly) | 2,000 |
| Fixed costs (monthly) | ₹1,50,000 |

---

## Q1: Unit Economics

### Calculations:

**Gross Margin per Unit:**
- Gross Margin = SP - COGS
- Gross Margin = ₹800 - ₹280 = **₹520 per unit**

**Amazon Fee per Unit:**
- Amazon Fee = SP × Fee %
- Amazon Fee = ₹800 × 12% = **₹96 per unit**

**Ad Cost per Unit (for ad-attributed sales):**
- Ad Cost per Unit = Total Ad Spend ÷ Units Sold via Ads
- Ad Cost per Unit = ₹3,00,000 ÷ 1,500 = **₹200 per unit**

**Net Margin per Unit (for ad-attributed sales):**
- Net Margin = Gross Margin - Amazon Fee - Ad Cost per Unit
- Net Margin = ₹520 - ₹96 - ₹200 = **₹224 per unit**

**Net Margin per Unit (for organic sales):**
- Net Margin = Gross Margin - Amazon Fee (no ad cost)
- Net Margin = ₹520 - ₹96 = **₹424 per unit**

---

## Q2: Monthly Profitability

**Total Ad-Attributed Revenue:**
- Revenue = SP × Units sold via ads
- Revenue = ₹800 × 1,500 = ₹12,00,000

**Total Organic Revenue:**
- Revenue = SP × Organic units
- Revenue = ₹800 × 2,000 = ₹16,00,000

**Total Monthly Revenue:**
- Total Revenue = ₹12,00,000 + ₹16,00,000 = **₹28,00,000**

**Total Units Sold:**
- Total = 1,500 + 2,000 = 3,500 units

**Monthly Profit Calculation:**
- Profit from ad-attributed sales = Net Margin (Ad) × Units (Ad)
  - = ₹224 × 1,500 = ₹3,36,000
- Profit from organic sales = Net Margin (Organic) × Units (Organic)
  - = ₹424 × 2,000 = ₹8,48,000
- Total Contribution = ₹3,36,000 + ₹8,48,000 = ₹11,84,000
- **Monthly Profit = ₹11,84,000 - ₹1,50,000 (Fixed Costs) = ₹10,34,000**

**Profitability Margin:** ₹10,34,000 ÷ ₹28,00,000 = 36.9% - **Highly profitable**

---

## Q3: ACOS and TACOS

**ACOS (Advertising Cost of Sale):**
- ACOS = (Ad Spend ÷ Ad Revenue) × 100
- ACOS = (₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹12,00,000) × 100 = **25%**

**TACOS (Total Advertising Cost of Sale):**
- TACOS = (Total Ad Spend ÷ Total Revenue) × 100
- TACOS = (₹3,00,000 ÷ ₹28,00,000) × 100 = **10.71%**

### Health Assessment:

**Is this healthy?**
- **ACOS of 25%:** This is **very healthy**. Most skincare brands aim for 15-30% ACOS. At 25%, GlowNest is in the ideal zone where ad spend generates ₹4 in revenue for every ₹1 spent on ads (implied ROAS of 4.0). The brand is not over-spending to acquire customers.

- **TACOS of 10.71%:** This is **excellent**. TACOS being low (well below 15%) indicates that:
  - The brand is building organic equity (48% of revenue is organic despite paid advertising investment)
  - The business is not dependent on paid ads
  - As organic revenue grows, profitability improves without increasing ad spend
  - The channel is sustainable long-term

**Key Insight:** The gap between ACOS (25%) and TACOS (10.71%) reveals that 48% of revenue is organic. This is the gold standard for e-commerce advertising—the brand is attracting customers beyond those clicking ads, which compounds over time.

---

## Q4: Break-Even Ad Spend

**Break-Even Analysis:**

The business becomes unprofitable when Monthly Profit = 0.

Currently:
- Fixed Costs = ₹1,50,000
- Organic profit contribution = ₹424 × 2,000 = ₹8,48,000

For the business to break even, the ad-attributed contribution must equal zero after covering fixed costs.

**Organic contribution (₹8,48,000) - Fixed Costs (₹1,50,000) = ₹6,98,000 buffer for ad operations**

If we set up the equation where ad operations go negative:
- Ad Contribution = (Net Margin per Ad Unit) × Units via Ads
- Ad Contribution = [(₹800 - ₹280 - ₹96) - (Ad Spend ÷ Units)] × Units
- Ad Contribution = [₹424 - (Ad Spend ÷ 1,500)] × 1,500

For total profit to equal zero, ad-attributed contribution must be negative enough to offset the organic buffer.

**However, a more practical approach:** 

Break-even occurs when ad spend reaches a point where net margin per ad unit becomes zero:
- Net Margin = Gross Margin - Amazon Fee - Ad Cost per Unit = 0
- ₹424 = Ad Cost per Unit
- Ad Cost per Unit = ₹424
- **Maximum Ad Spend = ₹424 × 1,500 = ₹6,36,000/month**

**Corresponding ACOS at Break-Even:**
- ACOS = (₹6,36,000 ÷ (₹800 × 1,500)) × 100 = (₹6,36,000 ÷ ₹12,00,000) × 100 = **53%**

**Reality Check:**
- Current ad spend: ₹3,00,000 (25% ACOS)
- Break-even ad spend: ₹6,36,000 (53% ACOS)
- GlowNest has **2.12x headroom** before the ad-attributed channel becomes unprofitable

## Q5: Scenario - Ad Efficiency Drops (ACOS worsens to 40%)

**New Parameters:**
- ACOS worsens to 40% (from 25%)
- Units sold via ads drop to 1,200 (from 1,500)
- Organic stays the same: 2,000 units
- Fixed costs unchanged: ₹1,50,000

**Calculating back from ACOS:**
- ACOS = 40% = (Ad Spend ÷ (SP × Units via Ads)) × 100
- 0.40 = (Ad Spend ÷ (₹800 × 1,200)) × 100
- 0.40 = (Ad Spend ÷ ₹9,60,000) × 100
- **Ad Spend = ₹3,84,000** (increased from ₹3,00,000)

**New Unit Economics:**
- Ad Cost per Unit = ₹3,84,000 ÷ 1,200 = ₹320 per unit
- Net Margin (Ad) = ₹520 - ₹96 - ₹320 = **₹104 per unit**
- Net Margin (Organic) = ₹424 (unchanged)

**New Profitability:**
- Profit from ad-attributed sales = ₹104 × 1,200 = ₹1,24,800
- Profit from organic sales = ₹424 × 2,000 = ₹8,48,000
- Total Contribution = ₹1,24,800 + ₹8,48,000 = ₹9,72,800
- **Monthly Profit = ₹9,72,800 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹8,22,800**

**Impact:**
- Previous profit: ₹10,34,000
- New profit: ₹8,22,800
- **Profit decline: ₹2,11,200 (-20.4%)**
- New profitability margin: ₹8,22,800 ÷ (₹800 × (1,200 + 2,000)) = 25.6%

### Recommendation:

**This is NOT a disaster, but action is needed.**

The 20% profit decline is significant, but GlowNest's business remains highly profitable at 25.6% margin. However, efficiency is deteriorating.

**Recommended Actions:**

1. **Investigate the root cause immediately (Week 1):**
   - Is external (competitor activity, category saturation, algorithm changes)?
   - Is internal (poor targeting, wrong keywords, weak creatives)?
   - How quickly did this happen? (Sudden or gradual?)

2. **Short-term actions (Week 2-3):**
   - Audit keyword performance; pause underperforming keywords
   - Test new ad creatives; old creatives may have ad fatigue
   - Optimize bid strategy; shift from broad match to exact match
   - Review ASIN/product selection; focus budget on high-converting products

3. **Medium-term decision (Week 4):**
   - If efficiency cannot be recovered, **reduce ad spend back to ₹3,00,000** and focus on organic growth
   - If efficiency is recoverable (industry standard for skincare), invest in account restructuring
   - Monitor organic sales; if organic is still growing, the brand is building long-term equity despite paid efficiency drop

4. **Communicate with client:**
   - "Your ACOS increased to 40%, which reduced monthly profit by 20%. Here's why this happened [root cause]. Here's our action plan [weekly milestones]. If we cannot restore efficiency within 30 days, we'll recommend reducing ad spend to protect profitability."

## Q6: Scenario - Scaling Up (Double Ad Spend)

**New Parameters:**
- Ad spend increases to ₹6,00,000 (from ₹3,00,000)
- Units via ads increase to 2,500 (from 1,500, NOT double due to diminishing returns)
- Organic sales unchanged: 2,000 units
- Fixed costs unchanged: ₹1,50,000

**New Unit Economics:**
- Ad Cost per Unit = ₹6,00,000 ÷ 2,500 = ₹240 per unit
- Net Margin (Ad) = ₹520 - ₹96 - ₹240 = **₹184 per unit**
- Net Margin (Organic) = ₹424 (unchanged)

**New Revenue and Profitability:**
- Ad-attributed revenue = ₹800 × 2,500 = ₹20,00,000
- Organic revenue = ₹800 × 2,000 = ₹16,00,000
- **Total revenue = ₹36,00,000** (up from ₹28,00,000)

- Profit from ad-attributed sales = ₹184 × 2,500 = ₹4,60,000
- Profit from organic sales = ₹424 × 2,000 = ₹8,48,000
- Total Contribution = ₹4,60,000 + ₹8,48,000 = ₹12,08,000
- **Monthly Profit = ₹12,08,000 - ₹1,50,000 = ₹10,58,000**

**ACOS and TACOS:**
- ACOS = (₹6,00,000 ÷ ₹20,00,000) × 100 = **30%**
- TACOS = (₹6,00,000 ÷ ₹36,00,000) × 100 = **16.67%**

**Comparison:**

| Metric | Current | Scaled | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ad Spend | ₹3,00,000 | ₹6,00,000 | +100% |
| Units via Ads | 1,500 | 2,500 | +67% |
| Ad Revenue | ₹12,00,000 | ₹20,00,000 | +67% |
| Monthly Profit | ₹10,34,000 | ₹10,58,000 | +2.3% |
| ACOS | 25% | 30% | +5% |
| TACOS | 10.71% | 16.67% | +6% |
| Profitability Margin | 36.9% | 29.4% | -7.5% |

### Analysis: Is This a Good Decision?

**The Math:**
- Profit increases only ₹24,000 (+2.3%) despite doubling ad spend (+100%)
- ACOS deteriorates from 25% to 30% (outside ideal range)
- TACOS approaches 15% threshold, leaving little room for error
- Profitability margin drops from 36.9% to 29.4%

**The Verdict: NOT RECOMMENDED in this form.**

**Why this scaling is inefficient:**

1. **Diminishing Returns:** Doubling ad spend only increased units by 67%. This suggests you've already captured the most efficient customers. Additional spend reaches less-qualified audiences.

2. **Profitability Risk:** The business is less resilient. A single 10% efficiency drop at scale (new ACOS = 40%) would be catastrophic.

3. **Margin Compression:** From 36.9% to 29.4% is a 7.5% margin loss. That's ₹2.1Cr lost on ₹36Cr revenue annually.

### Alternative Recommendation:

**Instead of scaling ad spend, scale differently:**

**Option A: Invest in Organic Growth (Recommended)**
- Keep ad spend at ₹3,00,000
- Invest the "extra" ₹3,00,000 into:
  - Enhanced product photography and A+ content
  - Review generation campaigns
  - Price optimization testing
  - New SKU development
- Expected outcome: Organic units grow from 2,000 to 2,500-3,000 over 3 months
- Benefit: Each additional organic unit carries ₹424 margin (vs. ₹184 at scale in paid)
- Result: Same revenue growth, but 31% higher profitability per unit

**Option B: Selective Campaign Scaling**
- Scale only top-performing campaigns (highest ROAS, lowest ACOS)
- Expect: Unit volume increases to 1,800-2,000 (not 2,500)
- Ad spend increases to ₹4,00,000-4,50,000 (not ₹6,00,000)
- ACOS stays 26-28%
- Profit increases by ₹80,000-120,000 on incremental spend of ₹1,00,000-1,50,000

**Option C: Pause Scaling, Focus on Efficiency**
- Keep current structure
- Use Q2 to test:
  - Sponsored Brand Video (typically higher ROAS)
  - Programmatic Ads (lower CPC)
  - New product launches (fresh demand)
- If efficiency improves to 20% ACOS, then consider 30-50% spend increase

### Final Recommendation to GlowNest:

"Your current unit economics are excellent. Doubling ad spend would grow revenue by 29% but grow profit by only 2% and reduce profitability margin by 7.5%. **Do not scale ad spend right now.** Instead:

1. Spend the next 60 days optimizing existing campaigns to improve ACOS toward 20%
2. Launch one new campaign format (e.g., Sponsored Brand Video) to test additional demand
3. Invest in organic growth drivers (content, photography, reviews)
4. Revisit scaling when organic units hit 3,000+/month or when you can demonstrate ACOS improvement to 22%

If you scale strategically, we can grow revenue 50% over 6 months while maintaining profitability margin above 35%."

## Key Takeaways for GlowNest:

1. **Current profitability is exceptional** (36.9% margin). This is NOT a mature business; it's a rapidly scaling one.

2. **Organic sales are the long-term profit engine.** 48% organic revenue means the brand is building equity. Every month of advertising trains the algorithm for future organic sales.

3. **Break-even is far away.** Ad spend can increase 2.12x before the channel becomes unprofitable. This is buffer, not permission to scale.

4. **Scaling requires operational excellence.** Doubling spend only added 2.3% profit. This suggests the team should focus on optimization, not just volume.

5. **TACOS is the metric that matters for long-term health.** ACOS can fluctuate; TACOS reflects whether the business is building an asset or renting visibility.
